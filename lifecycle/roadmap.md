# Roadmap

Every project has a **Roadmap page** showing its plan as a timeline of milestones. The roadmap is owned by the whole team: it can only change through a governance vote.

## Milestones

A roadmap is a list of milestones. Each milestone has:

- A **title** and a **description**
- A **target month**
- A **status** — Planned, In Progress, Completed, or Dropped

## Changing the Roadmap

To change the roadmap, a member opens **Propose Changes** from the Roadmap page. The editor starts from the current roadmap, and the member adds, removes, or edits milestones.

The resulting **roadmap proposal** is voted on by the DAO. Voters see a summary of what the change does — how many milestones are added, removed, changed, or left untouched — so they know exactly what they are approving. A proposal that changes nothing is refused, since there would be nothing to vote on.

When the proposal passes, it becomes the project's current roadmap. Roadmap proposals don't create tasks or move funds — they record what the team has agreed to aim for.

## Refining a Roadmap Proposal

Like backlog proposals, a roadmap proposal can be **refined** while it is still up for vote: a member submits an improved version, and if it passes, it replaces the original. The summary of changes is then shown against the proposal being refined, so voters compare like with like. Once a roadmap proposal has been executed, it can no longer be refined — a new change is simply a new roadmap proposal.

## What the Page Shows

- **The current roadmap** as a timeline
- **A banner** listing roadmap changes currently up for vote, each linking to its ballot
- **Past versions** — every earlier roadmap the team adopted, so you can see how the plan evolved
- Before any roadmap proposal has passed, the free-text roadmap from the project's founding application is shown instead

## Rewards

A validated roadmap proposal is an idea like any other: the people who shaped it can share an [Idea Reward](idea-rewards.md).
